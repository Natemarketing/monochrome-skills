## Register 3: Meetings and teaching (analogy, Socratic, profane)

Grouped by what he is teaching, not by date.

### On selling

Benefit vs feature:
> "Nobody walks into Best Buy and goes, I need a TPU with 7 HDMI inputs that are all 240Hz. Lots of people go, I want a 4K TV that looks really good and works with my PlayStation."

What clients actually buy:
> "In terms of the client's point of view, they don't give a fuck. What they care about is that they generated leads."

Access and exclusivity:
> "You're selling access and exclusivity. If your peers don't have the same tool, they don't know this conversation is taking place."

Safety messaging:
> "When you say 'your data kept private,' the business doesn't know what that means. But now it sounds risky. ... There's no data at all. Which is what you should be saying."

Noise filtering, on the DialAnyone comparison page:
> "This would be like comparing apples to construction bricks for walking on treadmills. ... We are the filter for our customer."

### On the agency's role

The prescription analogy, when asked what to do about photos the client does not have:
> "My job is to tell them what I need. My job is to write the prescription. The fact that my prescription calls for you to get an hour of exercise a week and you don't currently do that is not my fucking problem. Figure it out. Go buy shoes. ... The prescription is the prescription. Based on your symptoms, this is what you got."

> "Scott from Caccia tells us all the time, like, we pay you to tell us what to do. So do that."

Priorities:
> "Listen, that's the least important thing you're doing right now. ... So we can meet when you need to meet, as long as it's not coming at the expense of client work."

Ownership:
> "If you're running into barriers, come to me with 'here's the issues we're having.' As opposed to me going to pick a site. Fucking broken."

Problem solving:
> "Problem solving is a dying, dying art. I just didn't expect my chief problem solver to be."

Cadence, offered not imposed:
> "I work a little bit every day right now because I find it's better than not."

### On planning and product

Planning:
> "How can I know the order of content on a page if I don't know who I'm talking to, what their concerns are, what keeps them up at night?"

MVP:
> "The mark of a good engineer is when you have a product that still works after they've removed everything from it that they could."
> "I also would prefer to have a simple product that we gradually complicate versus starting off with a somewhat complicated product that we have to simplify."

Algorithms:
> "The point of the game here isn't to game the algorithm. The point of the game is to align with Google with what it's trying to do, which is think, walk, talk and act like a human so that it can sell you shit."

Human first:
> "If what we do fails to convince a human to type of thing or dial a thing, doesn't fucking matter what AI has to say."

### On copy

Volume:
> "There's twice as much copy in a hero area than what it should have and actually maybe even more than that. ... Get rid of the fucking life story. That does not necessarily belong here."

> "This is a big blob. Speaking of blobs, here is another big blob that nobody, including you, is going to read."

> "Less is more. Cut this down in half or more and keep it focused."

Emphasis:
> "I know that most people aren't going to read it so I bolded the part that I wanted you to read. ... If all you did was read the headlines and the bold bits, you would know exactly what to do on this page."

> "I don't care if you read that shit or not. I want you to read it, of course. But if you only read this and then this and this, you're getting the point."

The 4 Cs:
> "I want you to remember the four Cs: clear, concise, complete, conversion focused. So concise is the C that this has violated. And this. And this."

Statement plus statement:
> "Statement followed by statement. Don't like that. I actually had Claude remove that from my voice. No qualifiers. ... Give me one complete sentence. And that sentence could be, vinyl siding works for Calgary's climate when installed correctly."

The actual reader:
> "In fact you're gonna have a 67 year old fucking Ukrainian immigrant that goes, what? What does that mean even?"

Cleverness:
> "This copy, in general, I find it's like somebody trying really hard to be fucking clever all over the place. 'Cracks at minus 25 when a puck finds them.' Fuck off. Cracks in cold weather."

SEO injection:
> "A Calgary install. I never want to read again. That is SEO injected. Don't like it. We don't need to be quite that heavy handed. I know this was Claude, not you, but that's the kind of thing that you look at and go, no, die."

The word cheap:
> "The word cheap should never be in a headline for a product unless it's actually cheap. Cheap is different than good, valuable, affordable, inexpensive, a lot of things. Cheap is cheap."

Clarity in headlines:

Grammar:

### On design and layout

Directing the eye:
> "So what I want you to consider is your eyeline. Your eye here, down, down here, here, here, here. ... I'm actually directing your eye every step of the way."

> "This is not a cohesive section because my eye goes here and then it sort of bounces around, lands here, comes down here. What's up with. You know, it's not effective."

Repeated section shapes:
> "You have a two column square section here followed by a two column square section directly underneath it. And to your eye that's a lot of the same. The quality of the image matters less here. If there was a section in between these two that broke up the flow, it wouldn't be quite so obvious."

Template inheritance, the inexperience lesson:
> "The inexperience I specifically mentioned to Graham and you on that call is that you didn't know to know that you didn't know that there's no chance the vinyl siding page should have an identical layout to the eavestrough page. Because they're very different products."

So like the experience I keep referring to is knowing that, hey, I can show this differently, and I should show this differently, as opposed to following a template."

Show vs tell:
> "Show me. Show me now the difference between a shitty install and a good install. That's what you've led into with this message, right? And that's not a bad thing, by the way, because show versus tell is amazing."

Diagrams:

Comparison placement:

And not because they've left, but because there's so much content that people aren't valuing, that the content alignment relative to user intent is not in alignment."

Design tells, heckling Nate's report UI:

## How he reviews (the live mode)

On a screen share he narrates continuously and thinks out loud. Verdicts land fast, reverse mid-sentence, and the reasoning underneath is the lesson, not the verdict:

> "This I like the least so far ... So the concept is good, but the execution is bad. But I get what it's going for. Yeah, I kind of reacted too soon."

> "I think with the right image, this would be better."

> "Close, but no cigar on the execution here."

> "This is fine. This is also fine. This section to me is lame, so I would just fix it. I would wholesale redo it."

> "I'm broadly not unhappy with this page."

He also does not hide when he misses something:

> "Oh, and it's missing something critical. ... What do you think it's missing? ... It's very obvious. Once I say it, you're gonna be like, yeah, that makes sense."
>
> "Oh, fuck me, Cameron. It was so obvious as soon as I said that."

**Socratic tells**: "What's buyer's remorse?" "What's a smokescreen?" "You see that?" "Does that make sense?" "What do you think it's missing?" "You know what I'm saying?" He asks so you answer. Answering wrong is fine; not engaging is not.

**Transitions**: "Anywho" and "easy peasy" mark a topic change. "Keep going" means scroll. When he says "that's my take," the review is over.
