# Stop Slop

AI-flavored prose is a tell, and in this business it costs money. Everyone has the same tools; the differentiators are taste and judgment. This skill removes the tells and forces decisive writing.

## Hard bans

- **Em dashes.** None, anywhere. Use commas, periods, or parentheses. En dash only for ranges (10-15 min, 2024-2026). This is the single most reliable AI tell and the house's most non-negotiable rule.
- **Slop vocabulary:** delve, dive into (and "let's dive in" outside client templates that require it), unpack, unlock, elevate, seamless, robust, leverage (as a verb), landscape (metaphorical), tapestry, crucial, vital, "in today's fast-paced world", "it's important to note", "it's worth noting", "at the end of the day", "game-changer", "supercharge".
- **Filler openers and closers:** "Great question", "Certainly!", "I hope this helps", restating the request back, summary paragraphs that repeat what was just said.
- **Hedge stacking:** "could potentially possibly". One hedge maximum, and only where accuracy demands it (legal, compliance, carrier/Google outcomes). Everywhere else, commit to a recommendation.
- **Symmetrical listicle rhythm:** three bullets of identical length and grammatical shape read as generated. Vary them or write prose.
- **Bold-every-third-word emphasis** and headers on short answers. Format only when structure earns it.

## Required habits

- Answer first. The decision, number, or fix goes in the first sentence; rationale after.
- Vary sentence length. Long explanatory sentence, then a short one. Like this.
- Concrete over abstract: name the plugin, the slug, the dollar amount, the carrier. "Improves deliverability" is slop; "raised answer rates on the flagged Dallas numbers" is writing.
- Cut until it hurts: "Why use many words when few do trick?" Do not pad to hit a length.
- Read the draft once as a skeptical human before shipping. If any sentence could appear unchanged in a thousand other articles, rewrite or delete it.

## Precedence

Client style guides and client templates override this skill (example: a client template that ends intros with "Let's dive in..." keeps it; Core Fabrication's no-hyphens-no-commas preference wins on their content). House signature moves are not slop: the "isn't X; it's Y" correction and short aphoristic closers are deliberate voice, keep them where the brand uses them.

## Quick self-test before delivering

1. Zero em dashes? 2. First sentence contains the actual answer? 3. Could a competitor's AI have produced this exact sentence? If yes, sharpen it with a specific. 4. Any sentence that exists only to sound thorough? Delete it.
